# Breeding Grounds

Breeding Grounds are sacred communal sites of the [[Sa'ueia]]. They are permanently occupied mainly by infants, infirm adults, caretakers, rotating specialists, and allied organisms.

## Social Role

They are not cities. They are reproductive commons, legal anchors, memory reservoirs, medical centers, and ritual thresholds.

Adults return to breed, recover, teach, mourn, settle disputes, and renew obligations. Most then leave again with their family units.

## Caretaker Handover

*Ghostlight elaboration for `eclipse-nursery-handover`.*

Where a breeding ground meets a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]], routine handover happens at an open arrival terrace rather than inside the nursery hollows. Incoming specialists wait beyond a visible fungal boundary while outgoing caretakers exchange portable archives, inspect medical grafts on low work cradles, and declare sick tissue, damaged tools, and unresolved route debts. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern trees]] along the terrace rim provide eclipse light and can mark invitation or refusal, so the handover is witnessed by the commons' living partners as well as by Sa'ueia families.

The road may brighten a clean lane into the nursery, open a side lane toward an isolation shelter, or raise a ring of bitter fruiting beads around doubtful arrivals. No family owns that decision. Caretakers can offer truthful testimony, clean material, route credit, and temporary separation; forcing the boundary would turn a reproductive commons into property held by whoever can overrun its immune response.

## Last-Return Rights

A breeding ground's fixed location is an immunological liability. Its resident tissues become exquisitely familiar with local parasites, foods, wounds, and partner signals while the continent changes beyond them. Portable testimony can describe those changes, but a [[Technologies/Sa'auei'a Mobile Technologies|route archive]] cannot supply living symbiont lineages that have survived them. A ground can be well fed and still become biologically provincial.

To keep newborns compatible with distant routes, the commons periodically needs viable [[Ecology/Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis|memory-endosymbiont]] populations from adult Sa'ueia and allied organisms that have lived through ecologies missing from the nursery's recent experience. The preferred source is a body after death, when taking a lineage no longer weakens a living traveler's own immunity. The scarce input is not corpse-meat. It is survived difference.

Accepted remains pass first through a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]] outside the nursery boundary. The road and the ground's immune partners dismantle tissue in stages, attempting to destroy or sequester autobiographical echoes while preserving symbiont lines that carry useful repair, dietary, pathogen, and compatibility histories. The separation is imperfect. Over-hungry roads, [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Mirror Amoebae|mirror amoebae]], and poorly bounded grief can turn an inoculation into a dead person's partial replay inside nursery tissue.

A last-return right is a living adult's non-transferable grant giving one breeding ground first claim to those deep symbiont-bearing tissues after death. The grant is carried in the family's archive and witnessed by both a receiving caretaker and the road expected to process the body. It is a contract to offer, not an order to admit: the road may later reject contamination, and the commons may refuse lineages it already holds in dangerous abundance. The family retains custody of the person and their memorial record. Nobody gets to turn a promise about decomposition into ownership of the living body, however badly an accountant's mouth waters.

The promise nevertheless has present value. A ground short of particular route histories may advance graft maintenance, convalescent space, specialist teaching, scarce travel cultures, or material for a [[Civilizations/Sa'ueia Family Units|family unit]] whose adult makes the grant. Symbiont cultures cannot simply be copied forever: outside a whole surviving body they drift toward nursery conditions and cease to prove compatibility with the ecology that made them valuable. Grounds therefore compete for repeated last returns from underrepresented routes, and some subsidize perilous journeys because a traveler who comes home old is useful while a traveler who comes home dead is still an asset.

This produces an ugly class pressure inside a society that praises care. Widely traveled adults can secure advantages for their families by pledging biologically rare deaths. Permanent caretakers perform indispensable living labor but usually carry local symbiont histories the ground already possesses. Their last returns are treated as common inputs. Respect and bargaining power split: the people who keep the commons alive may be least able to borrow against dying there, while poor mobile families accept dangerous distance obligations to increase the eventual value of a body.

Without a steady exchange of survived difference, children remain healthy near their birth ground but become harder for remote routes, foods, grafts, and immune ecologies to admit. Family mobility contracts. Care populations accumulate. A reproductive commons can become a capital without anyone building a wall; the continent merely becomes too biologically foreign for its children to leave.

## Open Questions

- When a coastal traveler has promised corpse return to a [[Biosphere/Aquatic Colony Feeders/Choir Reefs|choir reef]] and a last return to an inland breeding ground, which claim can be divided without destroying what either partner was owed?
- Can imperial contact make a traveler's symbiont history simultaneously priceless and inadmissible, letting the empire starve Sa'ueia mobility by contaminating the returning dead rather than attacking nurseries?
- What leverage do permanent caretakers gain when they refuse to process rare last returns until the commons stops pricing their own deaths as biologically ordinary?

## Constraint

If a breeding ground becomes a capital, the Sa'auei'a system starts breaking. Permanent concentration would invite hierarchy, resource exhaustion, ecological resentment, and imperial-style capture.
