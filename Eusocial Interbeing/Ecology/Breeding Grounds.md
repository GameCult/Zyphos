# Breeding Grounds

Breeding Grounds are sacred communal sites of the [[Sa'ueia]]. They are permanently occupied mainly by infants, infirm adults, caretakers, rotating specialists, and allied organisms.

## Social Role

They are not cities. They are reproductive commons, legal anchors, memory reservoirs, medical centers, and ritual thresholds.

Adults return to breed, recover, teach, mourn, settle disputes, and renew obligations. Most then leave again with their family units.

## Caretaker Handover

*Ghostlight elaboration for `eclipse-nursery-handover`.*

Where a breeding ground meets a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]], routine handover happens at an open arrival terrace rather than inside the nursery hollows. Incoming specialists wait beyond a visible fungal boundary while outgoing caretakers exchange portable archives, inspect medical grafts on low work cradles, and declare sick tissue, damaged tools, and unresolved route debts. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern trees]] along the terrace rim provide eclipse light and can mark invitation or refusal, so the handover is witnessed by the commons' living partners as well as by Sa'ueia families.

The road may brighten a clean lane into the nursery, open a side lane toward an isolation shelter, or raise a ring of bitter fruiting beads around doubtful arrivals. No family owns that decision. Caretakers can offer truthful testimony, clean material, route credit, and temporary separation; forcing the boundary would turn a reproductive commons into property held by whoever can overrun its immune response.

## Eclipse Reserves and Trophic Credit

*Ghostlight elaboration for `ledger-breeding-ground-eclipse-reserves-v0`.*

A breeding ground concentrates the bodies least able to follow food: infants, infirm adults, graft patients, and the caretakers bound to them. Their demand peaks through eclipse, when warmth and fresh producer energy both narrow. Mature grounds therefore keep shallow, root-lined reserve basins beside their arrival terraces. The basins hold a warm mineral-sugar culture for nursery feeding, graft maintenance, and compatible microbial renewal. It is not supplied by one crop or one owner.

[[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Prismwake Mats|Prismwake mats]] release sugar-rich tissue only while their regrowth contracts remain solvent. [[Biosphere/Grazers and Browsers/Glassback Grazers|Glassback herds]] carry that tissue between wetlands and the commons, lend heat from their dorsal plates, and leave dung and shed material at road-safe collection beds. [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle fungal roads]] digest the clean waste and failed grafts into mineral liquor, screen the flow for dangerous memory residues, and open or close the intake channels. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Lantern trees]] contribute root sugars and eclipse light while sheltering the basins from thermal loss. Families repay the chain with mat repair, calving-lane defense, parasite pruning, spore carriage, clean dead matter, root protection, and skilled reserve work.

The scarce niche is not food in the abstract. It is a low-contamination intake seam where producer sugar, decomposer minerals, mobile heat, water, and witnessed route history overlap. Each ecological partner can reduce its own contribution, so neither a caretaker nor a prestigious family can spend the whole reserve by declaration. Nursery caretakers own triage among residents; the mats, herd, road, and grove retain authority over the flows they provide.

This produces class pressure without a minted currency. Families with long, well-attested return histories can ask several partners for credit at once and receive nourishment before repayment. Newly divided families, displaced families, and families whose archives were lost may bring equal skill or greater need but must offer labor and material up front while every contribution is sampled. Reserve keeping is prestigious because it interprets several living accounts at once; it is dangerous for the same reason. If the same keepers or old-return families monopolize that interpretation, trophic credit hardens into inherited access and the breeding ground begins becoming the capital it cannot afford to be.

When a reserve is overdrawn, failure travels outward: the road dims its intake channels, mats make their next harvest bitter, herds choose another calving lane, lantern trees keep sugars for their own roots, and graft work loses warmth and clean culture. Protecting the basins can keep the nursery alive for one eclipse while making the next supply cycle poorer. Every ration decision is therefore also a negotiation with whoever must feed the commons tomorrow.

## Constraint

If a breeding ground becomes a capital, the Sa'auei'a system starts breaking. Permanent concentration would invite hierarchy, resource exhaustion, ecological resentment, and imperial-style capture.
