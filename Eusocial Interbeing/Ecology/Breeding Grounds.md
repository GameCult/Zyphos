# Breeding Grounds

Breeding Grounds are sacred communal sites of the [[Sa'ueia]]. They are permanently occupied mainly by infants, infirm adults, caretakers, rotating specialists, and allied organisms.

## Social Role

They are not cities. They are reproductive commons, legal anchors, memory reservoirs, medical centers, and ritual thresholds.

Adults return to breed, recover, teach, mourn, settle disputes, and renew obligations. Most then leave again with their family units.

## Caretaker Handover

*Ghostlight elaboration for `eclipse-nursery-handover`.*

Where a breeding ground meets a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]], routine handover happens at an open arrival terrace rather than inside the nursery hollows. Incoming specialists wait beyond a visible fungal boundary while outgoing caretakers exchange portable archives, inspect medical grafts on low work cradles, and declare sick tissue, damaged tools, and unresolved route debts. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern trees]] along the terrace rim provide eclipse light and can mark invitation or refusal, so the handover is witnessed by the commons' living partners as well as by Sa'ueia families.

The road may brighten a clean lane into the nursery, open a side lane toward an isolation shelter, or raise a ring of bitter fruiting beads around doubtful arrivals. No family owns that decision. Caretakers can offer truthful testimony, clean material, route credit, and temporary separation; forcing the boundary would turn a reproductive commons into property held by whoever can overrun its immune response.

## Heat Pledges and Mobility Debt

*Ghostlight elaboration for `ledger-stillwarm-allotment-v0`.*

At the Stillwarm Shelf, a provisional descriptive name for one sub-Umbros breeding ground, the scarce resource is not warmth in general but heat that is steady, clean, and close enough to a nursery hollow for infants and infirm adults to survive the daily eclipse without spending their own reserves. Low stone-and-root cradles hold that heat and condition temperature-sensitive medical grafts for mobile families; during shortage, every warmed packet is visibly competing with a body. [[Biosphere/Grazers and Browsers/Glassback Grazers|Glassback herds]] charge the cradles by resting their translucent heat-storage plates against the outer ribs after feeding.

The exchange reaches across the local trophic web. The breeding ground protects calving lanes, prunes herd parasites, supplies mineral washes, and stakes its standing with [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Prismwake Mats|prismwake mats]] for reliable graze. The glassbacks turn mat sugars and returned light into mobile warmth. [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle fungal roads]] carry minerals into the cradle roots, digest dung, spoiled food, and failed grafts, and close beds whose waste or disease load exceeds what they can safely absorb. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Lantern trees]] keep the work lanes visible during eclipse, but their cold light cannot settle a chilled infant's metabolism.

No family can buy a warmer cradle. Caretakers allot clean heat by bodily need, while the herd, road, mats, and grove retain their own refusals over what they will feed, carry, illuminate, or touch. When a pledge falls short, able-bodied adults lose comfort first, then departure windows. A family whose grazing damage, concealed sickness, or unpaid route debt helped cause the shortfall may be required to remain for mat repair, fungal sorting, herd grooming, or extra watch until the commons' credit is restored. This is called mobility debt: care remains unconditional, but leaving on time does not.

Mobility debt is meant to keep shortage from becoming infant triage or private ownership. It also creates class pressure. Families remembered well across many routes can recruit witnesses and discharge obligations quickly. Families with poor standing, bad luck, or few surviving partners may spend so long doing stationary repair that rotation becomes inheritance. A breeding ground begins to fail politically before it fails thermally when the same bodies repeatedly become its permanent infrastructure.

## Constraint

If a breeding ground becomes a capital, the Sa'auei'a system starts breaking. Permanent concentration would invite hierarchy, resource exhaustion, ecological resentment, and imperial-style capture.
