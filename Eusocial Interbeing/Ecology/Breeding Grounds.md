# Breeding Grounds

Breeding Grounds are sacred communal sites of the [[Sa'ueia]]. They are permanently occupied mainly by infants, infirm adults, caretakers, rotating specialists, and allied organisms.

## Social Role

They are not cities. They are reproductive commons, legal anchors, memory reservoirs, medical centers, and ritual thresholds.

Adults return to breed, recover, teach, mourn, settle disputes, and renew obligations. Most then leave again with their family units.

## Caretaker Handover

*Ghostlight elaboration for `eclipse-nursery-handover`.*

Where a breeding ground meets a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]], routine handover happens at an open arrival terrace rather than inside the nursery hollows. Incoming specialists wait beyond a visible fungal boundary while outgoing caretakers exchange portable archives, inspect medical grafts on low work cradles, and declare sick tissue, damaged tools, and unresolved route debts. [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern trees]] along the terrace rim provide eclipse light and can mark invitation or refusal, so the handover is witnessed by the commons' living partners as well as by Sa'ueia families.

The road may brighten a clean lane into the nursery, open a side lane toward an isolation shelter, or raise a ring of bitter fruiting beads around doubtful arrivals. No family owns that decision. Caretakers can offer truthful testimony, clean material, route credit, and temporary separation; forcing the boundary would turn a reproductive commons into property held by whoever can overrun its immune response.

## Pulse Niches and Draw Debt

*Ghostlight elaboration for `ledger-breeding-ground-pulse-niche-v0`.*

A breeding ground can persist only where several kinds of abundance overlap on the same schedule. Sa'auei'a call the usable overlap a pulse niche in trade translation: returning light reaches producer tissue; [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern trees]] have stored enough sugar to light and shelter the nursery; a [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal road]] can exchange clean decomposition for minerals; and nearby herds, wetlands, or coastal partners can supply heat, spores, salts, or other locally missing matter. The niche is the synchronized relationship, not a substance that can be loaded into one pack.

Mobile families maintain that overlap from outside the commons. They protect calving and bloom corridors, carry spores and mineral concentrates, return bodies and failed grafts cleanly to decomposers, repair route damage, escort specialists, and accept long detours when a partner closes. Caretakers turn the arriving flows into warm hollows, nutrient pastes, medical graft baths, and memory-safe recovery work for infants, reproductive tissue, and infirm residents. Couriers carry warnings and credit between the partners, but cannot replace the bulk matter or hands.

A **draw** is one claim on the next usable pulse: some combination of stored heat, food, clean graft capacity, specialist attention, and ecological permission. It is recorded in portable archives as sources, witnesses, unmet obligations, and expected repair work rather than as a single price. Caretakers can schedule and declare a draw; they cannot mint one. The road can withhold minerals, trees can darken shelter, mats can close sugar pores, and herds can move their heat elsewhere. Access therefore has several vetoes and no solitary owner.

Existing family debt cannot by itself justify abandoning an infant or infirm resident. Under shortage, caretakers protect dependent care first and delay elective breeding, adult recovery, archive copying, or ceremonial use. That rule prevents debt from becoming a death sentence, but it does not remove class pressure. Families remembered across many routes can assemble contributions, witnesses, and substitute partners quickly enough to use the safest reproductive windows. Thin-standing families wait longer, travel farther, and take more hazardous maintenance obligations. A route-rich family may describe this advantage as generosity receiving its due; a route-poor family may accurately describe the same arrangement as inherited access wearing the manners of reciprocity.

Overdrawing a pulse produces a cascading shortage rather than an empty shelf. A hungry road returns fewer minerals, weakened trees ration light and shelter, producer tissue becomes less generous, herds choose another corridor, and the following pulse arrives smaller. Repair can take seasons because each partner must be repaid in the material and behavior it recognizes. If caretakers hide those costs, reserve the best pulses for allied lineages, or make permanent office out of temporary scheduling power, the breeding ground begins turning route credit into a resident class. That is one of the ways a commons starts becoming the capital it cannot survive being.

## Constraint

If a breeding ground becomes a capital, the Sa'auei'a system starts breaking. Permanent concentration would invite hierarchy, resource exhaustion, ecological resentment, and imperial-style capture.
