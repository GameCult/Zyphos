# The Unclosed Roost

*Canon candidate. Hollow Reach and the individual names below are provisional descriptive names.*

At Hollow Reach, a small lantern-tree grove on an Umbros-facing slope of the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]], a returning threadwing can find its old nesting fork still available. Beneath the fork, a knot of cold light gives the invitation it knew before leaving. Other couriers have fed there, sheltered beneath it, and brought news. None has been allowed to build in that particular place.

Several [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern trees]] here keep such roosts. Their memory-bearing trunk tissue already preserves nesting agreements; the local practice is to continue one after regular visits stop, without requiring the missing partner to have promised a return. A tree can reserve one fork while admitting new nests elsewhere. Some keep none. Some reopen a fork after an unsuccessful flowering season. One tree, called Crooked Cup for its low divided branch, keeps a place through repeated absences even when other pollinators offer useful work in exchange for it.

For a [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|threadwing courier]], the pleasure is physical: a familiar landing approach, a sheltered place for ribbonlike sensory vanes, nectar from a known provider, and no immediate contest over where to settle. Regular visitors preen nearby and bring pollen. Young couriers following experienced travelers learn which invitations they may answer and which belong to someone else. An apparently empty fork can be the least available place in the canopy.

Trees gain reliable partners by making a return easy. That explains much of the practice, but does not settle why Crooked Cup repeatedly favors this particular absentee over an available replacement. Visitors sometimes call it attachment. What they can observe is a sustained choice: the same invitation, the same nesting permission withheld from others. No one has read an explanation out of the deep trunk.

## What the Light Does Not Tell

An unclosed roost attracts stories. Its former occupant may have died, found better feeding, changed its route, or simply ceased bringing news. The invitation says that the tree will receive it. Visitors nevertheless repeat it as evidence that a return is expected. A tree's hospitality gradually becomes somebody else's prediction.

At Hollow Reach, experienced couriers discourage carrying that prediction onward. They also distinguish a report of having encountered the absentee from a report that it intends to come back. They practice the distinction by carrying reports of encounters while declining to pass on predictions drawn only from the waiting light.

News reaches the grove through pollen, courier-borne traces, and reports exchanged with [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal roads]]. Each source knows different things. A road may know that traffic has shifted without knowing what one animal wants. A courier may recognize the missing individual at another roost without knowing whether it will stay. Here, refusing to supply a destination is tolerated more readily than falsely reporting a death. A living partner can have reasons to keep its whereabouts from becoming route gossip.

Crooked Cup's absentee has now been encountered elsewhere. The courier carrying that news, Split Vane, also knows the new approach would be attractive to other travelers. Delivering the route along with the encounter could bring traffic the absent courier never invited. Withholding everything leaves Crooked Cup receiving increasingly mournful reports from visitors who have seen only the empty fork.

Meanwhile, a new threadwing pollinator has begun landing beside the reserved place and bringing pollen. It repeatedly approaches the fork after feeding. Crooked Cup illuminates another branch. The newcomer leaves without nesting, then comes back with another load.

## Hooks Left

- Split Vane can carry evidence of the encounter while withholding the route. Will Crooked Cup keep the roost longer once absence no longer suggests death?
- The new pollinator begins building on the offered branch, but brings nesting fibers toward the old fork whenever it approaches. Crooked Cup must continue refusing, or change the invitation.
- The absentee returns to feed and departs again before the nesting invitation ends. Its old fork remains empty beneath the light.
