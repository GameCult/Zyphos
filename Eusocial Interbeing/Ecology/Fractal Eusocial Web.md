# Fractal Eusocial Web

Life on Zyphos stores and exchanges memory at many scales. Cells regulate incoming memories, organisms remember the terms of feeding and travel, and larger living communities control reproduction, archives, and territory. These relationships form the fractal eusocial web: a network in which similar questions of access, trust, and obligation recur within bodies and between them.

“Contracts” here are remembered terms governing an exchange. They can concern food, signals, shelter, ancestry, or passage. A cell, wound, herd, route, reef, breeding ground, and nation can each decide what to admit and what to refuse. That gives each a kind of politics without making them the same kind of mind. The scales fit inside and connect to one another; they do not form a ladder toward ever more sophisticated societies.

## Scale Bands

### Cellular and Microbial

Zyphos life belongs to `Memoriobionta`: organisms whose cells contain mutable memory endosymbionts, internal living partners that carry memories which can change. `Memorioplast` is the provisional name for the endosymbiont itself.

At this scale, compatibility determines what a cell allows into its life. Cells retain some memories, trust some neighbors, repeat some signals, and destroy some intrusions. Local assemblies of cells, described as `Congressus cellularis`, coordinate repair, scar tissue, sensory emphasis, hunger, and inherited responses.

Memory also moves between hosts. `Amoeba specularis` describes the mirror-amoeba pattern: small mobile bodies copy nearby memory states and keep replaying them. A host, pond, gut, or root sheath can then accept, edit, or reject the signal.

### Tissue and Organ

Within a body, immune behavior determines the terms on which tissue can belong. Closing a wound involves negotiation over the repair. A graft can receive partial acceptance: it may be admitted for nutrition while being kept apart from ancestry memory, or required to carry a local oath before it can integrate more deeply.

`Litura immunis` is a placeholder name for these tissue rites. They determine what counts as sufficiently “self” for a particular body, season, injury, and lineage. Acceptance depends on those circumstances; it need not be a single permanent decision.

### Organism

An organism has some independence, but its cells and living partners still coordinate the work of keeping it alive. Legs, roots, shells, and wings carry these committees into the wider ecology.

Current examples include:

- `Stroma prismatica`, the [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Prismwake Mats|prismwake mats]], keep grazing records in wetland light.
- `Arbor laternaria umbrosa`, the [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern trees]], keep eclipse routes open and collect taxes on their use.
- `Ala filaria`, the [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|threadwing couriers]], transport memory-bearing pollen, symbionts, route reports, and social injuries.
- `Pascuavitreus socialis`, the [[Biosphere/Grazers and Browsers/Glassback Grazers|glassback grazers]], exchange heat and communicate status through translucent dorsal plates.
- `Flos onerarius`, the [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|burden flowers]], attach to travelers and carry gossip about immune states.

None is solitary in the Earth sense. An organism can travel beyond others’ reach, but distance does not make it a wholly separate form of life.

### Colony and Route

A colony-scale being forms when many bodies take on stable roles as organs of a larger whole. Together they sustain its use and exchange of memory.

`Formica reticulata` describes the [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Lattice Ants|lattice ant]] pattern. Bodies, shed fiber, and microbial glue assemble into mobile diagnostic circuits. These circuits read the history of the ground and sell the results to whoever feeds them. A sugar payment can therefore expose information to someone who was never meant to have it.

`Via mycelialis candelaria` describes the [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle-road]] pattern. These fungal transport systems remember payment, sickness, trampling, shelter, and reproductive access.

`Chorus liminalis` names [[Biosphere/Aquatic Colony Feeders/Choir Reefs|reef and waterway colonies]]. Their borders follow current, pressure, mineral taste, larval drift, and storm memory.

### Archive, Commons, and Nation

At larger scales, living beings can preserve archives, sustain reproduction, or act over a territory. These are distinct responsibilities:

- Archive-scale beings retain memories across periods longer than most mobile species can personally verify.
- Commons-scale beings maintain reproductive continuity.
- Nation-scale beings exercise territorial agency.

`Silva mater archivora` is the mother-tree archival nation. It may provide food, weather memory, and healing to visitors while refusing to let them copy deep ancestry memories. Receiving care does not grant access to the archive.

`Area generationis communis` is the breeding-ground commons. Its members include caretakers, infants, infirm adults, symbionts, defensive organisms, and memory partners. Together with remembered law, they constitute a reproductive institution rather than a nursery owned by one species.

`Pratum pactivorum migrans`, `Mycelioviae candelariae`, and `Chorus liminalis` are native nation-scale forms. Each sets borders according to what can pass through without undoing its identity. Their different answers produce different kinds of territory.

## Relation Channels

The web can be represented as a graph: beings are nodes, and their relationships are connections between nodes. The following labels describe broad kinds of connection. They should become more specific as Weksa naming and Aquarium simulation needs develop.

- `stores`: a being retains memory in a form it can use locally.
- `copies`: memory is duplicated, echoed, or distorted as it enters a new body.
- `bounds`: a membrane, immune rule, ritual, or route determines what traffic may pass.
- `feeds`: beings exchange matter and energy under remembered terms.
- `reports`: a mobile organism carries information about immune, emotional, or legal states.
- `routes`: one being serves as another’s means of transit.
- `diagnoses`: a being reads local history and sells, gives, or weaponizes that knowledge.
- `scales`: a pattern of organization forms a larger political body.
- `anchors`: a source of reproductive or archival continuity maintains a mobile society’s good standing.
- `authors`: engineered memetics imposes a structure of relationships instead of negotiating it.
- `invades`: one system of boundary control attempts to overwrite another.
- `misreads`: imperial categories fail to describe an ecology with no single root from which the whole can be understood or controlled.

## The Imperial Topology

*Imperium airavense clausum* belongs in this web because it is alive at nation scale. Its internal organization, however, departs from the native pattern.

Natural interbeings allow local authorities to disagree. A valley may forgive what a river refuses. A mother archive may accept a marriage while rejecting a copied genealogy. A fungal road may feed a traveler and quietly report them to a breeding ground with better standing. Acceptance by one part of the web does not settle what another part will do.

The empire’s [[Biological Memetic Engine]] creates a strict internal structure. All permitted variation serves imperial goals. Its border blocks uncontrolled memory traffic, and its agents receive exactly enough autonomy to remain maximally productive.

From a native Airawa perspective, the disturbing feature goes beyond tyranny. The empire is a living system organized in a foreign way, wearing ancestral symbols and presenting those symbols as proof that its organization is natural.

## Simulation Notes

Aquarium can model these relationships without giving every organism its own agent. It needs to represent the terms of exchanges, how readily things can cross boundaries, and the scales at which beings act.

Useful starting variables are:

- For each node: memory capacity, energy need, boundary policy, and trust state.
- For each connection: channel type, permeability, debt, and contamination risk.
- For eclipse cycles: which connections become active, and which become more expensive.
- For imperial connections: uniform behavior and high throughput, with brittleness when unfamiliar local categories appear.
- For native connections: slower, messier exchanges with more redundancy, making the network harder to seize through a single root.

An organism’s appearance should remain connected to the work it performs. The network model should likewise show who controls exchanges and passage, because those relationships are the ecology’s politics.
