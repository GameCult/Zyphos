# Benefactor Capture

Benefactor capture is the enclosure of a living commons through prolonged overfeeding. It is most feared at [[Breeding Grounds]], where a family or allied trophic bloc can give so much useful nourishment that the commons' bodies learn the donor as a condition of health.

No land changes hands. No granary needs a lock. The appetite becomes the property line.

## Mechanism

Under [[Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis]], nourishment never arrives as chemically anonymous fuel. Sugars, mineral pastes, decomposed tissue, microbial cultures, and medical feed carry residues of the organisms that made, routed, and handled them. Repeated safe use teaches recipient tissues which metabolic signatures predict repair rather than invasion.

A route-rich [[Sa'ueia Family Units|Sa'ueia family]] can coordinate prismwake sugars, fungal minerals, carried symbionts, and preserved tissues from the same circuit of partners. If those gifts dominate several growth cycles, infant microbiomes, caretaker grafts, nursery filters, and resident allies begin to prefer their shared signature. [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle roads]] grow toward the dependable waste stream. Local immune systems lower their scrutiny of familiar cultures. Substitute food remains nutritious but increasingly provokes poor uptake, quarantine, or rejection.

The supplier is usually not one species pretending to be a merchant house. A family, road, producer sheet, grazer herd, and microbial lineage may all benefit from the exclusive traffic and defend it for different reasons. Their coordination turns a remembered gift into a trophic bloc.

## The Benefactor's Leverage

Capture is difficult to distinguish from exemplary generosity while it is being built. The gifts keep infants alive, reduce caretaker labor, feed allied organisms, and rescue the ground during shortages. Refusing them imposes certain harm now to avoid a dependency that may take years to become visible. Some benefactors begin with no plan beyond helping and discover the power only after withdrawal becomes dangerous.

Once established, the bloc can condition continued supply on nursery priority, route judgments, admission of particular dependents, favorable dispute testimony, or reproductive access. The breeding ground remains formally communal, but alternatives now look biologically unsafe. A donor can therefore threaten famine without owning food stores and exercise custody without ever claiming the commons as property.

Route-poor, newly divided, and displaced families suffer twice. Their smaller contributions cannot retrain the ground, and rejection of their unfamiliar food is cited as ecological proof that they lack standing. Permanent caretakers absorb the immune crises, rationing blame, and slow work of keeping alternatives viable. Benefactor families pay real surplus over long periods, maintain their partner bloc, and risk losing the entire investment if one indispensable species defects.

Grounds that resist capture deliberately rotate sources, cap continuous donor share, and preserve metabolically awkward minority foods. This costs growth, creates mild immune stress, and can offend organisms offering genuinely abundant help. [[Reciprocal Ecologies]] therefore acquire an ugly corollary: sometimes a commons must refuse generosity in order to remain capable of receiving anyone else's.

## Open Pressures

- When a sentient road, mat, herd, or microbial lineage inside a benefactor bloc wants to leave, is it defecting from a contract or liberating a commons it helped capture?
- Who may authorize the dangerous lean interval required to retune dependent infants and caretaker tissues, and which bodies are expected to bear that immune cost?
- Can the [[Airawa Empire]] offer a supply culture broad enough to rescue a captured ground while quietly making imperial nourishment the next monopoly?
