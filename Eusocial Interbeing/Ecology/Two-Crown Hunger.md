# Two-Crown Hunger

Two-Crown Hunger is an alliance practice in which rival [[Species/Matriarch Trees|Matriarch Trees]] make a mobile keystone guild metabolically dependent on both of them. The trees do not exchange hostages. They cultivate a constituency whose ordinary feeding route is the treaty.

## The Metabolic Hinge

Each Matriarch and her symbiotic feeders produces one half of a complementary ration. The first half causes a guild member's memory-bearing gut symbionts to store an abundant nutrient in a chemically locked form. The second half teaches those same symbionts to release it while replacing a trace compound consumed by the release. Either grove can fill a stomach. Only alternating between them completes the diet.

The arrangement is grown slowly through selective feeding, microbial exchange, nesting access, and developmental exposure rather than imposed by a single device. Under [[Ecology/Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis|mutable memory endosymbiosis]], young raised on the route inherit enough of the learned digestive state that the dependence can persist after the founding adults die. Pollinators, spore carriers, carrion guilds, grazers, and other mobile partners can all become hinge guilds when their work is important to both food webs.

A guild has reasons to accept. Its members gain protected feeding at two canopies, defended breeding sites, priority passage, and the power to withhold a service from either side. The guild is not livestock. Once its route becomes load-bearing, it can call both Matriarchs to account by arriving late, carrying less, or stopping at the border with both halves of the ecology waiting on it.

## Alliance as Annexation

Two-Crown Hunger makes peace self-enforcing and rivalry intimate. A tree that closes the route starves the hinge guild, but also loses whatever the guild pollinates, disperses, clears, hunts, diagnoses, or reports. [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle Fungal Roads]] can read the altered waste of an underfed guild, and [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwing Couriers]] can make a delayed ration public before either Matriarch has finished calling it a crop failure.

The dangerous contest is dependency depth. Each Matriarch tries to make the hinge guild indispensable to more of the rival's ecology than to her own. She may shelter its broods during a bad eclipse season, let alternative service guilds decline, or retune her half-ration so the next generation needs more frequent visits. A successful tree acquires a faction inside the rival polity without moving a root: local producers, scavengers, Airawa households, and route bodies begin lobbying for foreign access because their own continuity now depends on it.

This produces two recurring blocs within mother-tree polities. Cross-crown constituencies defend permeability, mixed symbionts, and the hinge guild's right to negotiate as a polity in motion. Single-root constituencies seek a locally complete diet, either to end foreign leverage or to restore a Matriarch's control over her dependents. Both can claim to be protecting autonomy. One protects the guild from either tree's monopoly; the other objects that descendants are born owing a journey their founders chose for them.

## Failure and Capture

Withholding one half of the ration is a sanction with a trophic fuse. The hinge guild weakens first, then the services around it fail: unfertilized producers, uncleared carrion, uncarried spores, unpruned parasites, and darkened report routes push costs onto organisms that never negotiated the original alliance. A Matriarch can therefore injure a rival by starving her own clients, but she cannot control where the cascade stops. Low available energy makes maintaining redundant service guilds expensive, which is why the trap survives even when everyone can describe it.

The [[Civilizations/Disconnected Tree Networks|Disconnected Tree Networks]] face a particularly ugly choice. A two-crown route can bind isolated resistance ecologies into a coalition, yet the inherited compatibility that keeps the guild alive can also reopen a biological path for the [[Technologies/Biological Memetic Engine|Biological Memetic Engine]]. Imperial agents offer locally complete rations as liberation from Matriarch dependency. The offer is materially persuasive; whether the new digestive independence carries imperial authorship is the part usually printed in living tissue rather than on the invitation.

Two-Crown Hunger is therefore both [[Themes/Ecological Bargaining|ecological bargaining]] and a border technology among [[Ecology/Nation Scale Interbeings|nation scale interbeings]]. It creates alliances that cannot be reduced to signatures, because the signatories include the appetite, offspring, predators, routes, and refuse of a living constituency.

## Open Questions

- If a hinge guild finds a third ecology able to complete its diet, is that emancipation, treaty theft, or the founding of a new nation scale route body?
- When one Matriarch dies, what obliges a disputed successor to maintain her half-ration before the hinge guild's broods begin to fail?
- Can a hinge guild deliberately retune its own symbionts and reverse the dependency, making two Matriarchs compete to remain edible?
