# Nation Scale Interbeings

A nation scale interbeing is a living system made up of several species that together hold territory, preserve memory, and can act as a political whole. These are the largest ordinary political organisms on Zyphos. Calling one a nation describes what the organism can do; it is not a metaphor.

An outsider encounters its authority through practical permissions: whether they may travel through it, feed there, reproduce, receive care, or exchange memories. The interbeing governs what crosses its tissues, routes, archives, shared reproductive resources, and the places where its defenses meet the outside world. How open each of those boundaries is—and to whom—constitutes its foreign policy.

## Boundary Permeability

An interbeing's border is spread across living systems rather than drawn as a single line. What it admits depends on what kind of traffic it can survive. Permission to cross through one channel does not settle permission to use another.

The channels include:

- nutrients;
- access to reproduction;
- permission to use routes;
- passage for predators and scavengers;
- visiting symbionts;
- pathogens and systems that perform immune-like functions;
- memory exchange;
- copies of archival material;
- carriers of taboos, rituals, and law;
- military organisms and engineered memetic packets.

Most native nation scale interbeings apply different rules in different places. A marsh front may admit fungal couriers while blocking grazer herds. A cliff nesting system may welcome Sa'auei'a midwives and turn away Airawa archive pilgrims. Even within one negotiation, a mother-tree polity may agree to share weather memory while withholding ancestry memory.

Diplomacy therefore proceeds through specific permissions, often slowly, and takes effect in living bodies and habitats. A treaty may establish a pollinator route, suspend an immune response temporarily, grant guests access to breeding grounds, or set a fungal toll. A remembered injury may close a valley for three generations.

## Native Forms

### *Silva mater archivora*

Mother-tree archival nations are rooted, densely interconnected systems that hold extensive memory. Trees, mycorrhizal networks, insects, bird-analogues, and Airawa partners together preserve ancestry, ecological precedent, repair protocols, and legal memory.

Entry into the territory does not imply access to those records. These nations often admit food and rumor readily while tightly controlling archival copying. Foreign bodies may enter, trade, mate, and heal without ever being allowed near the deep memory organs.

### *Pratum pactivorum migrans*

Migratory pasture nations move along routes, and their territory changes shape over the year. Herds, soil films, seasonal bloom corridors, scavenger guilds, mineral licks, and mobile families together sustain the nation as a moving body.

Their boundaries travel with those bodies. Invitations determine who may join or enter, closures restrict access, and debt registers preserve obligations along the way. A fixed map misses this movement and misrepresents the territory it claims to show.

### *Mycelioviae candelariae*

Fungal-road nations begin with the work of connecting places and supplying them. Their networks have become sovereign systems, joining groves, breeding grounds, shelters, wetlands, and corpse gardens through dim routes that carry nutrition and memory.

They generally welcome travelers, but keep unforgiving accounts. A traveler who feeds the road may receive passage, warnings, and shelter. One who cheats it may find that every safe path has become unavailable.

### *Chorus liminalis*

Choir reef nations live along coasts and river mouths, in drowned forests, and in eclipse-dark shallows. Their boundaries follow pressure, currents, mineral taste, drifting larvae, and seasonal migration. Dry land provides little guidance for locating their edges.

Their negotiations concern harbor access, shelter for spawning, storm predictions, and concealment from predators. Memory carried by swimmers and filter colonies is also part of what they negotiate.

## The Imperial Exception

*Imperium airavense clausum* is an artificial nation scale interbeing built by the [[Airawa Empire]] through the [[Biological Memetic Engine]]. It belongs to the provisional order `Clausiformes`: interbeings organized around sealed or nearly sealed boundaries. It did not grow naturally as part of the native patchwork.

The imperial system deliberately blocks uncontrolled memory traffic at its boundaries. Within them, it permits exactly as much autonomy, local variation, and cellular creativity as its agents need to remain maximally productive. Its deadly effectiveness depends on that selective freedom: each part may move and create precisely where doing so serves the whole.

Tribal Airawa and native interbeings experience the empire as alien because its internal relationships no longer resemble an ecology whose participants negotiate with one another. It still contains rituals, lineages, memories, and species partners. All have been re-authored to serve a single artificial system of usefulness. That system venerates the blood and memes of its founding tribe, even as it becomes something the tribe could not have understood.

## Working Taxonomic Placeholders

The following names are provisional, Latin-heavy handles for describing relationships among these forms of life. They use familiar taxonomic conventions as a design scaffold; they do not imply Earth ancestry. They remain placeholders until Weksa can render culture-specific names from local languages.

- Domain: `Memoriobionta`, memory-endosymbiotic life.
- Superclade: `Contractilia`, organisms whose survival depends on explicit memory-bearing ecological contracts.
- Clade: `Polybionta`, multispecies bodies with stable internal role differentiation.
- Class: `Regnobionta`, nation scale interbeings with territorial agency.
- Order: `Permeabiliformes`, naturally grown interbeings with graded borders.
- Order: `Clausiformes`, interbeings organized around sealed or nearly sealed boundaries.
- Family: `Archivoridae`, mother-tree-centered archival polities.
- Family: `Viatoridae`, migratory route polities.
- Family: `Mycoviariidae`, fungal-road polities.
- Family: `Chorallidae`, reef and waterway polities.
- Family: `Imperiidae`, engineered imperial superorganisms.

The example binomials and trinomials remain provisional and should stay italicized: *Silva mater archivora*, *Pratum pactivorum migrans*, *Mycelioviae candelariae*, *Chorus liminalis*, and *Imperium airavense clausum*.

## Design Use

When adding a large ecology or polity, establish how its authority works:

- Which kinds of traffic do its borders admit, limit, or reject?
- At what scale does authority over memory reside?
- How does someone gain reproductive access?
- How much autonomy do local parts retain?
- How does the interbeing punish betrayal?
- Which immune responses do outsiders mistake for political decisions?
- Which laws do outsiders mistake for natural conditions?
