# Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees

Umbros-facing lantern trees grow on slopes where [[World/Zyphos Umbros Binary System|Umbros]] dominates the sky. Cold bioluminescent knots hang beneath their canopies, lighting paths for small creatures below. They glow brightest as an eclipse begins. Their light cannot replace sunlight, but it is enough to keep small travelers moving through the dimness.

These trees also keep records and decide who may use their groves. They feed pollinators, shelter young grazers, regulate passage, and collect payment for their services. A lit path offers guidance under the tree's terms.

They are distinct from [[Matriarch Trees|Matriarch Trees]], although the two can form treaties. Lantern trees specialize in keeping routes visible, timing pollinator traffic, sheltering infants, and taxing the surrounding neighborhood.

## Light, Signals, and Memory

The lantern knots hang below the canopy rather than at its crown, directing light toward ground traffic. Their colored pulses carry messages as well as illumination. [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwing Couriers]], [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Lattice Ants|Lattice Ants]], and some Sa'auei'a route readers can distinguish invitations, closures, taxes, mourning, and warnings.

Inside the trunk, layers of memory-bearing growth tissue—memory cambium—hold records on different timescales. Outer rings store recent passage, wounds, pollen, and fungal reports. Deeper rings preserve lasting agreements and obligations: who may nest, which grove is quarantined, where a trail may cross, and which family line failed to repay shelter.

The tree gathers information through pressure on its roots, the taste of pollen, courier gut symbionts, fungal reports, chemicals released by distressed infants, and canopy temperature. Its archive therefore depends on both its own senses and what other organisms bring to it.

## What Passage Costs

During dangerous dim phases, lantern trees feed pollinator analogues and shelter young grazers. In exchange, they require travelers to follow the designated routes. Staying on a lit path avoids damage to roots, leaves useful trace material behind, and carries pollen or fungal spores onward. Route discipline is part of the payment.

Trees also cooperate with organisms that deliver spores, clean wounds, protect infants, and report disease honestly. Their kindness carries obligations, including debts that can outlast the immediate need for shelter.

[[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle Fungal Roads]], living fungal transport networks, often braid through lantern-tree roots. The tree supplies sugars and records of weather at different heights. The road supplies mineral reports, corpse recovery, and news of disease farther away.

Each partner can put pressure on the other. A road that lies can lose its local sugar supply and be starved by the tree. A tree that closes too many routes can find the road directing travelers and social activity elsewhere, leaving the grove behind.

## Cooperation and Closure

A lantern tree needs to harvest light, protect its roots, keep pollinators visiting, regulate safe passage, and prevent contamination of its archive. These needs guide its decisions about access.

Root cutting, false quarantine claims, unauthorized nesting, imperial sampling, and attempts to force access to deep memory can cause it to withdraw cooperation. Its responses include dimming paths, redirecting pollinators, calling in lattice ants to diagnose what has happened, and making its fruit sour.

Eventually, it may close the grove altogether. Darkness then marks a refusal of passage: the grove has become a legal barrier as well as an unlit place.

## Playing a Lantern Tree

A lantern tree NPC controls tolls and keeps children alive in the dark. Both responsibilities matter to how it behaves. It can be kind; its help is never free.

Track the conditions that can change its decisions:

- **Sugar reserve:** how much sugar it has available.
- **Route congestion:** how crowded its routes have become.
- **Archive anxiety:** how concerned it is about the safety of its stored memory.
- **Fungal credit:** its standing in exchanges with fungal partners.
- **Pollinator loyalty:** how reliably pollinators continue to serve it.
- **Imperial pressure:** the pressure imperial interests are placing on it.

Make its decisions visible through its body and the grove. It can pulse its knots, light a path, plunge that path into sudden darkness, tremble its roots, release pollen, or emit bitter mist. It can also illuminate one traveler while leaving the traveler beside them in darkness.
