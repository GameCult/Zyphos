# Prismwake Mats

Prismwake mats are thin, living sheets that spread across shallow marshes, mudflats, and slow floodwater. They harvest light to make sugars, and grazers depend on them for food. A mat remembers how visitors treat it: gentle feeding earns easier access to sugars on a later visit, while damaging bites can make the feeding surface bitter and slippery.

From a distance, a mat can look passive. Underfoot, it regulates who may feed and on what terms. Its memories and visible signals make it the wetland's first public ledger of visitor behavior.

## A Living Surface That Remembers

Each mat contains light-harvesting tissue, microbial memory partners, mineral pores, and contract cells tuned to the eclipse cycle. Its upper skin consists of prism cells. These open before the predictable eclipse-shadow and close after the light returns.

The cells remember warmth, pressure, bite shape, saliva chemistry, and the immune signatures of recent visitors. The mat also stores light-angle memory. During bright return phases, its surface flashes pale green, blue, rose, and colorless silver. These flashes communicate its accounting of encounters; they are not courtship displays.

Below the surface, the mat exchanges minerals with [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|Candle Fungal Roads]] and the root sheaths of [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees]]. Deep access to fungal roads lets a mat afford more generous feeding terms. A stranded mat demands more from its visitors.

## How Visitors Keep or Lose Access

[[Biosphere/Grazers and Browsers/Glassback Grazers|Glassback Grazers]] learn careful feeding early. A herd that damages prismwake tissue loses a reliable source of food for eclipse recovery.

Mats favor slow grazing, careful foot placement, and visitors who return after showing restraint on an earlier visit. Mineral gifts and fungal spore delivery also encourage cooperation. Gentle grazers receive sugars more readily on their next pass.

Tearing bites provoke bitter tissue, slippery folds, and warning flashes that [[Biosphere/Couriers and Pollinators/Threadwing Couriers|Threadwing Couriers]] can see. Repeated trampling, concealed disease, and attempts to mask identity with [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Mirror Amoebae|Mirror Amoebae]] also turn a mat against a visitor.

A mat's threat response escalates from local bitterness to slick surfaces, then to synchronized warning blooms. Those blooms recruit grazers, couriers, fungal roads, and nearby mats into excluding the offender.

Mats also receive reports about visitors. [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|Burden Flowers]] often cross on hosts and leave sweat-mineral signals carrying information about them. A mat treats this as testimony to weigh, rather than established truth. If a flower has a history of exaggerating danger, its host can face extra bitterness.

## What Guides a Mat's Behavior

A mat gathers light, protects its regrowth nodes, and tries to maintain reliable grazer traffic. It also needs to keep its fungal credit solvent: its obligations to fungal partners matter alongside the condition of its own tissue.

It remembers through pressure patterns, saliva chemistry, light angle, and microbial residues. Courier-carried warnings give it information beyond its own encounters. Together, these channels let it respond to both a visitor's behavior on the mat and reports brought by others.

## Using a Mat as an NPC

Play a prismwake mat as a territorial clerk with photosynthetic skin and excellent grudges. It cannot chase a visitor. It can change what that visitor is allowed to eat and how difficult the ground becomes beneath their feet.

Track the conditions that shape those decisions:

- **Light reserve and eclipse phase:** how much light it has stored and where it is in the eclipse cycle.
- **Trust toward the visitor:** how it regards the individual now crossing or feeding.
- **Fungal debt:** what it owes its fungal partners.
- **Regrowth injury:** the damage it needs to protect and recover from.
- **Disease alarm:** its current concern about disease.

Make its responses visible. A mat can brighten or dim, open sugar pores, sour its tissue, or fold away from feet. It can reflect a visitor's old color pattern back at them, or flash a complaint toward passing threadwings.
