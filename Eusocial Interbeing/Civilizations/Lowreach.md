# Lowreach

*Canon candidate. Locality and personal names are provisional.*

Lowreach is an [[Airawa]] community built around the exposed feeding shallows of a wetland on the [[Airawa Home Continent]], beyond imperial territory. Its houses stand on scattered dry rises. Between them, narrow climbing frames leave most of the water open to light. A resident can spend an afternoon within reach of three households without setting a foot in the marsh.

[[Prismwake Mats]] spread through the shallows, turning light into sugars for [[Glassback Grazers]]. The grazers carry fungal spores and leave dung for [[Candle Fungal Roads]]; those underground networks return minerals to the mats. Careful grazing preserves the feeding surface and earns easier access on later visits. Lowreach depends on this continuing traffic. Broad decks would shade the producers, while a causeway across the shallows would obstruct the herds that help sustain them. Here, residents have found that extending a house over a feeding patch reduces what that patch can support.

Airawa keep the crossings clear, remove storm debris, and carry usable household waste to fungal feeding sites. In return, dependable passage through the wetland brings upland traders who exchange food and replacement building material for repairs, hauling, and local guidance. Lowreach imports much of its own food; the mats sustain its partners rather than serving as an Airawa staple. A [[Matriarch Trees|matriarch tree]] on the wooded margin holds communal memory and gestates young under agreements with the residents and pollinators. The marsh is home, but birth and access to the communal past still depend on that relationship.

## Within Reach

Lowreach's best-loved rooms extend into the space between houses. An Airawa anchors taloned feet on a rough crosspiece, braces with the large upper arms, and uses the smaller lower hands to share food, groom a friend's scales, or mend something while listening. Resting slings support the body when holding on becomes tiring. The climbing frames widen at the dry rises, where small groups can linger without hanging above a feeding patch.

Residents admire hosts who can make this comfortable: a support in the right place, enough room for an awkward turn, the favourite resting angle remembered. Children first learn on low frames over dry ground. Being trusted to visit the next house alone is a considerable event, followed by several adults discovering urgent reasons to look that way.

Stories pass along these adjoining rooms. A teller may spend several visits describing one remembered journey, stopping whenever the listeners need to leave. Young people also invent lives for distant Airawa they have never met. Some aspire to travel; others want a house where travellers linger long enough to become particular people. Those who return bring new stories into the adjoining rooms.

Company is easy to find and difficult to escape. Turning one's body away is an ordinary request to be left alone, but it cannot prevent overhearing. Some households enjoy the continual talk. Others keep compact enclosed rooms on their dry rise and join the shared frames only when invited. The oldest households occupy the largest rises, giving them space to be both sociable and private. Their praise of openness can sound rather different from inside a crowded sling.

## A Room for Issik

Issik restores damaged climbing supports and shares a house with their sibling Hadrul. They have saved enough imported material to build a closed room. Issik wants to practise telling stories without receiving helpful endings from next door; they also want somewhere to grieve a dead companion without having their silence watched.

Their own rise is full. Extending over the mat would consume feeding light, so Issik wants to rent part of a larger rise farther along the crossing. Hadrul offers to rebuild the shared house instead. That would preserve their daily closeness, but cost Hadrul a beloved open room where neighbours have gathered for years. Hadrul likes living this way. Issik likes Hadrul.

Other residents want to pool material and make several small private rooms on the last unbuilt dry rise. Its current users favour keeping a broad resting place where elders and tired travellers can meet without climbing. Both uses would spare the mats. They cannot both occupy the whole rise, and households with spare rooms have been unusually generous with advice.
