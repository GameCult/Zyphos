# Company Perches

*Canon candidate. Locality and personal names are provisional, pending Weksa naming work.*

At Bent Bough, a small nonimperial [[Airawa]] neighborhood, some homes keep a perch beside the place where residents mend tools and groom one another. A visiting [[Threadwing Couriers|threadwing]] can settle within reach, have parasites picked from its ribbonlike sensory vanes, and stay while the household goes about its business. Residents call these company perches. They enjoy being sought out when there is nothing to send.

An Airawa anchors with taloned feet and braces with an upper arm while the small, blunt lower hands work through the vanes. The arrangement leaves room for the courier to move away. A familiar visitor may turn to present another patch, then leave the offered hand waiting while it investigates somebody else's work. Learning when to continue and when to stop is part of becoming good company. Children first learn to keep their hands still beside a landed animal; reaching is easier than being trusted with reach.

Bent Bough occupies a branch settlement beside an [[Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|Umbros-facing lantern grove]] on the [[Airawa Home Continent]]. Its residents maintain tools, tend the grove, and help sustain the pollination traffic that serves it. Their wider community keeps its communal memory with [[Matriarch Trees]]. Households provide clean stopping places and preening within the local exchange with couriers. The company perch brings some of that work into the room where people choose to spend their unoccupied time.

Some homes are prized for patient grooming. Others have an open edge from which a visitor can watch arrivals while remaining close to an Airawa. One resident likes a courier to stay within reach; another prefers the small interruption of its landing, looking about, and leaving again. Neither experience requires understanding a route song. Residents recognize particular animals by familiar vane patterns and habits, though fraying and regrowth sometimes make an allegedly certain identification less certain.

A courier has its own nest, mate, cargo, and route obligations. A household can be a favored stopping place within that life. At Bent Bough, invitations to live together often include an explanation of which visitors already use the home. An incoming resident may like the Airawa very much and find their morning company exhausting. Some couples maintain separate work ledges. Some keep the perch outside. Homes without one remain ordinary homes, although enthusiasts can be tedious about what everyone else is missing.

The difficult distinction is between being needed and being preferred. Ithu has cared for the courier called Split-Ribbon through many vane regrowths. Since Ithu's close companion Darem moved in, Split-Ribbon usually lands beside Darem first. Darem is an indifferent groomer but sits still for long stretches repairing small bindings. Ithu believes that stillness explains the preference. Darem suspects the courier simply likes Darem. Neither can establish the cause by watching where it lands.

Ithu still enjoys the exacting work of preening. What hurts is being called over to do it after the visitor has settled with someone else. Darem has begun describing Split-Ribbon as their shared companion, which sounds generous until Darem leaves the cleaning to Ithu. Ithu wants Darem to learn that work, and also wants some visits that do not end with being summoned across the room.

Neighbors differ over what care entitles someone to expect. Some think a beloved visitor's continued return is ample reward, wherever it settles. Others object that this advice usually comes from the person beside whom it settles. The custom persists because care is useful, familiar company is pleasurable, and repeated visits allow attachments to grow. It cannot arrange those attachments evenly.

## Hooks Left Open
