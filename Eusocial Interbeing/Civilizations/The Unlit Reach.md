# The Unlit Reach

*Canon candidate. Local names are provisional.*

At the Fallen Boughs, some traveling families save fibers discarded from their gear for a road they cannot yet walk. They leave the material at the edge of its amber candles, then return on a later journey to see whether the light has advanced. A few new beads beneath a fallen trunk can make an otherwise ordinary return worth talking about for years.

The Boughs are a scatter of lantern groves and deadwood hollows on the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]], before imperial contact. Several [[Biosphere/Decomposers and Recyclers/Candle Fungal Roads|candle fungal roads]] feed among them. These are underground networks whose fruiting bodies guide travelers; they digest discarded material and seek new corridors. Here, a road sometimes grows through deadwood well before it lights a continuous passage. Its body can reach somewhere that its guidance does not yet recommend visiting.

The [[Civilizations/Sa'ueia Family Units|Sa'ueia families]] who repeatedly pass through the Boughs have made a pleasure of supporting these unfinished reaches. Their finest boast is to have fed a road before it could offer them a journey. They remember the first broken tool it consumed and the hollow where its candles finally met the next grove's light. Young travelers learn the places through these stories, including the lovingly preserved accounts of reaches that went nowhere.

Some families prefer the dependable roads and spend their spare material keeping familiar passages healthy. Others become attached to a particular growing network. They hope it will eventually bring a favored mineral lick or another family's circuit within easier reach. Neither group stays permanently. Their relationships with the roads continue through returns, and an ambitious reach can occupy several generations of family conversation.

Growth is difficult to judge from above. A hungry road may be extending through new feeding ground, recovering from poor traffic, or simply failing. Its [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern-tree partners]] know their own sugar exchanges and receive fungal reports, but travelers do not automatically share that knowledge. Roads here often keep a developing branch dark while continuing to recommend an established passage. A family can know where it has supplied food without knowing where the fungus has spent it.

For that reason, local travelers watch their [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|burden flowers]]. These small plantlike companions cling by rootlets, remember fungal scents, and seek places to reproduce near useful routes. At some unfinished verges, a carried flower begins dropping seed beads. Enthusiasts take this as encouragement: perhaps the flower has encountered something their own observations missed. Experienced companions point out that flowers also shed reproductive material under threat. Seed on the ground does not settle the argument.

The flowers have their own immediate stakes. A familiar host brings light, minerals, grooming, and transport; a promising verge offers a chance to reproduce. A flower can lean, cling, bloom, or shed there without understanding a family's ambition for the whole corridor. Travelers admire companions whose offspring become established along a successful reach. They also swap embarrassing stories about confidence placed in a flower that was having a bad journey.

Now the road called Underfall remains hungry after several well-fed returns. One supporting family has seen seed beads fall beyond its last candles and quietly begun calling the reach nearly ready. That claim has traveled farther than the family. Another road, Broad Amber, already offers a reliable longer passage and continues attracting traffic and nourishment. Underfall's supporters want to show people the new growth before everyone gives up on it.

They could expose buried strands by lifting the deadwood, but doing so would disturb the feeding body they want to help. They could ask its root partners to share reports, revealing how little they themselves know. Or they could wait for Underfall to extend its recommendation, while a hopeful story hardens into a reputation for exaggeration. The road's continued darkness leaves all three choices open.

- A lantern tree offers to illuminate the deadwood from above. Will visitors understand that its light shows a place to observe, while Underfall still withholds passage?
- A family's flower has offspring on Broad Amber as well as Underfall. Its hosts must decide whether to admit that their favorite sign of promise favors both roads.
- Underfall's next candles appear toward a different grove from the one its supporters wanted to reach. The new connection may flourish without fulfilling their cherished prediction.
