# The Unfinished Duets

*Canon candidate. The locality and practice names are provisional translations pending Weksa work.*

At the proposed Foldwater inlet on the [[Sa'ueia Continent]], returning coastal families gather to play with a [[Choir Reefs|choir reef]]. A performer pushes a flexible chamber beneath the water, releases it, and waits. Pressure reaches the reef's anchored, polyp-covered skeleton; sometimes another pattern comes back. Listeners rest against portable sounding frames along the shallows. A good evening may contain very little movement and a great deal of waiting for someone to repeat the interesting mistake.

The families call these performances unfinished duets. Travelers carry their own parts inland, where the missing reef part becomes a pause. Someone encountering a duet far from the coast can learn its rhythm without knowing what prompted it, whether the reef answered, or why the performer stopped.

## Learning To Answer

These [[Sa'ueia]] families already travel through relationships renewed over generations. Foldwater is one return along their working routes, a place to repair equipment, bring mineral offerings, and meet acquaintances whose arrivals seldom coincide exactly. The gathering fits around that work. Nobody maintains a permanent audience on supplies meant for the nursery shelves.

The proposed instruments extend the portable communication crafts suggested in [[Sa'auei'a Mobile Technologies]]. Water chambers make pulses; frames convey vibration to a resting body through adjustable contact pads. They reproduce only selected features of a reef response. They neither transfer its memories nor give every listener the same sensation. A frame fitted to one traveler can make another lose the quietest passage altogether.

Apprentices begin by finding a comfortable contact, then distinguishing a pulse made by their teacher from water moving around the frame. Children enjoy hiding an extra beat in a familiar pattern. Experienced players admire someone who notices a small reply without making it larger for the audience. Others prefer vigorous pieces that can be felt through poorly fitted equipment. There is a continuing argument over whether delicacy has become an expensive taste.

The reef is a participant at colony scale. Individual polyps are not assigned solos or treated as little critics. Its selective repetitions suggest attention, but performers disagree about whether a particular return expresses pleasure, recognition, irritation, or something for which they have no word. They can improve their understanding through repeated visits without settling that question for every piece.

## What A Piece Is Allowed To Mean

Families give traveling versions names: an awkward arrival, a companion's recovery, the shelter that stayed open through a storm. These names help people remember and enjoy the work. They also tempt listeners to hear explanations where there are only interpretations.

One popular piece, provisionally called *Room For Another*, ends with a widening interval. Inland performers introduce it as the reef welcoming strangers. Its coastal maker knows that its first performance followed a difficult reconciliation within the family. The reef repeated the interval; no one established that it meant welcome. Explaining the title fully would expose another family member's private quarrel. Leaving it alone lets an attractive rumor acquire the weight of local knowledge.

Foldwater players commonly object to performing a duet as a request for passage. The reef's shelter, infection, and nursery requirements still govern visits. A familiar pattern cannot settle those matters. Some touring players nevertheless advertise themselves as teaching the reef's hospitality, and prospective travelers may plan around that claim.

There are several defensible responses. A maker can share the original circumstances with a trusted player, demonstrate the gaps between versions, offer a less suggestive title, or leave the personal story private while publicly rejecting the supposed travel advice. Fellow artists disagree about how much explanation a work deserves. Some bring careful accounts inland; others value the possibility of enjoying something whose maker remains partly unknown.

## Open Hooks

- A traveling family arrives expecting shelter because it learned *Room For Another*. Its teacher is present and still insists the interpretation is sound.
- A cheap new sounding frame makes the duets accessible to many more bodies but drops the faint pulse on which one celebrated interpretation depends.
- After several visits without a reply to a favorite piece, younger performers want to change their part. The original player wants to keep a place for the answer they remember.
