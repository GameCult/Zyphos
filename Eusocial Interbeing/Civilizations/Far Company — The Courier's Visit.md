# Far Company — The Courier's Visit

*Canon candidate. Names are provisional translations.*

At the foot of the nesting cliffs, a lantern tree lights the visiting Sa'ueia route reader and leaves the courier beside them dark. The reader waits. The courier, called Bent Ribbon here, has crossed this valley many times carrying other people's business. This time it has brought companions to meet someone it admires. Being thanked for finding the way would be a poor substitute.

The Far Company is a small association of [[Sa'ueia Family Units|traveling families]] and [[Threadwing Couriers]] who choose visits together. It gathers on an upland circuit of the [[Sa'ueia Continent]], before imperial contact, where family routes cross flights between lantern groves and nesting cliffs. Its members want to be received together, with each traveler's wishes counting toward what happens after arrival.

They enjoy comparing expectations with places. Sa'ueia companions describe the valley they imagine while couriers circle ahead on ribbonlike sensory vanes; returning flyers sometimes repeat an approach that once fooled a confident route reader about the size of a distant ridge. Families continue making and exchanging goods. Couriers take suitable deliveries. Visits fit around feeding, mates, nests, and keeping living cargo viable. Some members accompany only the ground stretch between two groves. Flying partners regroup with them rather than expecting ground travelers to follow an aerial shortcut.

## A Welcome They Can Read

The cliff-foot [[Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern tree]] already receives Bent Ribbon as a pollinator and carrier. Its invitation pulses are familiar to the courier and to this family's route reader. On an earlier visit, the tree admitted the reader's family while feeding Bent Ribbon at its usual stopping place. Neither food nor proximity settled whether the courier was included in the visit.

Now Bent Ribbon lands beside the family marker instead of at that stopping place. The reader remains there too. The tree illuminates the courier as well as the reader, repeats its invitation pulse, and lights the ground path into the grove. Both enter.

These particular partners take the exchange to mean that both may come in now. It conveys no itinerary, promise of future shelter, or permission to approach nests above the grove. Their company has obtained a shared welcome from one host; the cliff residents still decide their own welcome.

## What a Guest Gets to Choose

Bent Ribbon wants to remain for the return of a cliff courier whose route songs it repeatedly pauses to hear. Their meeting can take place on the exposed stone below the nests, within reach of the Sa'ueia companions. Bent Ribbon has repeatedly led them there and waited; they understand the desired visit through that familiar behavior, without claiming to know everything the songs mean.

The sponsoring family wants to leave earlier for a well-established exchange farther along its circuit. Received as an embassy, it would ask hosts about the family's next destination and send Bent Ribbon ahead as guide. Received as Far Company, it must also consider staying for Bent Ribbon's meeting. The hosts can approach the waiting courier directly; its companions can follow its visit for once. Guesthood changes whose anticipation deserves an afternoon.

The sponsor has a substantial stake. It has offered to keep its introduced ground travelers on the grove's lit paths. If they shortcut across exposed roots and wound the tree, it expects to remain, help tend the wounds under the tree's direction, and supply the nourishment it had undertaken to bring while passage recovers. Missing its next exchange would cost real goods and future confidence. [[Reciprocal Ecologies|Remembered treatment]] can affect later journeys, including journeys made after these visitors have gone elsewhere. The sponsor wants authority over departures because it has put its continuing relationship behind this arrival.

A smaller family offers to share that work and favors waiting. Direct acquaintance with the cliff residents would let it develop a route without always following its influential neighbor. Bent Ribbon's mate prefers the sponsor's dependable roost arrangements and would gladly take the earlier departure. Neither courier speaks for all couriers. The company persists because these connections make travel richer and sometimes more possible; each journey exposes how much of the choice its members will actually share.

## Unresolved Hooks

- The admired courier returns early but approaches Bent Ribbon alone. Will Bent Ribbon bring its ground companions into the encounter it wanted them to share?
- The smaller family offers to stay behind if roots are damaged. At the next approach, the tree illuminates the established sponsor and leaves the would-be substitute waiting.
- Bent Ribbon's mate accepts the onward delivery. The company must decide whether its hoped-for afternoon together is worth traveling separately afterward.
