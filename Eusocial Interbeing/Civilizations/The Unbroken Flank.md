# The Unbroken Flank

*Canon candidate. Locality and practice names are provisional.*

On the proposed Pale Step flats of the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]], a glassback can acquire a devoted following by making another glassback take one involuntary step. Two adults stand flank-to-flank, their translucent backs showing the living heat-storage tissue beneath. One suddenly flashes the plate pattern of panic. The other tries to keep contact, remain readable, and let the alarm pass without carrying it into the surrounding herd. A beautifully timed answer is a small movement: the receiver turns broadside to the watching animals, still touching the challenger, with its own plates clear.

The local name for this is the unbroken flank. At a gathering, the same two rivals may return to each other repeatedly while other adults graze, exchange warmth, or approach a performer they particularly want to touch. Admiration is visible in who chooses whose side afterward. Nobody has to count anything for a conspicuously deserted champion to notice.

[[Biosphere/Grazers and Browsers/Glassback Grazers|Glassbacks]] already share warmth and low-grade memory gradients through close arrays. Flank exchange carries affiliation and can dampen alarm; their plates can also darken or produce false panic flares. Here, several neighboring herds have made the tension between those capacities into a practiced pleasure. A receiver has two kinds of information at once: the alarming exposed pattern and the familiar body still pressed against it. Skill lies in attending to both. No participant can assume contact makes every warning false.

Pale Step is a stretch of bare mineral ground beside shallow feeding wetlands. These herds return for mineral films and nearby [[Biosphere/Primary Producers/Prismwake Mats|Prismwake Mats]], holding displays after feeding during bright recovery. Calves stay among nonparticipating adults. They copy broadside turns and tiny starts in ordinary contact; adults end a calf's attempt by turning away, rather than trying to frighten it harder. The practice offers a future young glassbacks can visibly pursue: becoming the body others approach with eager caution.

There are different accomplishments to want. Some performers produce a brief, convincing flare and become clear again before their rival moves. Others excel at receiving an awkward, lingering alarm without fogging their own plates. Some adults never challenge but repeatedly choose proximity to particular performers. Experienced receivers can be sought after without becoming route leaders. A grazer capable of defeating every familiar rival may still be appalling at choosing water.

Recently, a performer provisionally called Long Glint has begun turning outward during the flare. More of the gathering sees the false panic, and several bodies start at once. Long Glint draws an impressive crowd. Another performer, Dapple, answers only inward-facing challenges and breaks contact as soon as a rival turns out. Long Glint repeatedly approaches Dapple; Dapple repeatedly leaves. To watchers, this can look like either principled refusal or a very economical way of avoiding defeat.

The new fashion reaches beings who never joined the exchange. [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|Burden Flowers]] on startled spectators can broadcast the hosts' real distress. Nearby mats weigh those reports, and a sudden rush onto feeding tissue can provoke slippery folds and warning blooms. One outward display has already sent several spectators into the wet margin. The next visit finds the damaged mat bitter. Hunger makes further jostling more likely, precisely when careful grazing matters most.

Some followers now gather farther onto the bare flats, accepting a longer exposed walk to feeding water in order to keep the larger displays. Dapple's companions remain near the sheltered margin and refuse outward challengers. Neither gathering can stop flowers from reporting fear or guarantee that an unfamiliar herd recognizes play. The custom can persist through small, familiar encounters; its exciting new scale is what has become unstable.

Three things remain unsettled:

- Long Glint approaches Dapple beside the still-bitter mat, with hungry spectators already closing in. Dapple must decide whether to receive, withdraw, or turn broadside between the challenger and the wetland.
- A visiting herd begins its protective calf ring during an outward flare. Local performers have seconds to make their own movements intelligible before the herds collide.
- A celebrated receiver notices a disturbance beyond its rival. Breaking contact will look exactly like losing until something else moves.
