# Sa'ueia Family Units

Sa'ueia families travel together in squad-sized groups. Each group raises children, defends its members, makes things, trades, practices rituals, negotiates with ecological partners, and keeps memories. Kinship holds together work that elsewhere might belong to separate institutions.

The intended size allows members to know and trust one another while supporting specialized roles. A wolf pack is a useful comparison for how they move, hunt, and respond to kinship pressures. It explains only part of their life: these families also teach, act as embassies, run workshops, carry goods as caravans, and bind members through oaths.

A family's standing depends on good relations with many ecological partners—and on those partners remembering those relations. Hoarding, causing wounds, or breaking obligations along a route can cost a family that standing. Its members may remain physically able to travel, but the continent becomes less open to them. The precise form of that refusal is not specified here; the distinction is between being able to move and having the relationships that make a route available.
