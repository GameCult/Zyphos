# Sa'ueia Family Units

Sa'ueia family units are squad-sized mobile groups. They combine kinship, defense, childcare, craft, trade, ritual practice, ecological negotiation, and memory keeping.

## Scale

The unit should be small enough for personal trust and large enough for specialized roles. Wolf-pack comparison is useful for movement, hunting, and kinship pressure, but the units also function as schools, embassies, workshops, caravans, and oath groups.

## Social Logic

Families earn status by maintaining good remembered relations across many ecological partners. A family that hoards, wounds, or breaks route obligations may still physically travel, but the continent stops opening itself to them.

## First-Departure Care

*Ghostlight authored elaboration for `hearth-brood-companion-handover-v0`.*

Descent alone does not settle who belongs to a family unit. At a child's first departure from a [[Ecology/Breeding Grounds|breeding ground]], the outgoing caretakers hand over a bundle of care obligations: the child's body and route education, any continuing medical needs, and any allied organism whose established care contract would be damaged by separation. A trusted [[Biosphere/Parasites and Symbionts/Burden Flowers|burden flower]] that warned through infancy is therefore a participant in the handover, not luggage attached to the child.

The outgoing caretaker demonstrates the flower's mineral washing, light exposure, grooming, and warning history on a low work surface. The receiving family names who will perform each task on the next route and records the division in its portable archive. The child's preference, the flower's grip and color, and the caretaker's testimony are partial evidence; none is treated as a complete diagnosis or a private confession. If care capacity is uncertain, the family may redistribute cargo, divide its route, arrange a supported separation, or delay departure. It may not claim the child first and quietly discard the companion later.

Taking both bodies costs pack space, route time, minerals, and privacy. A hungry or neglected flower may exaggerate alarms, while suppressing a credible bloom can endanger the child and teach both dependents that family safety means silence. Repeated neglect is remembered by breeding-ground caretakers and route ecologies as a failure of the whole unit's claim to kinship; future children, companions, and medical dependents may be entrusted elsewhere until the family can demonstrate shared care again.
