# Sa'ueia Family Units

Sa'ueia family units are squad-sized mobile groups. They combine kinship, defense, childcare, craft, trade, ritual practice, ecological negotiation, and memory keeping.

## Scale

The unit should be small enough for personal trust and large enough for specialized roles. Wolf-pack comparison is useful for movement, hunting, and kinship pressure, but the units also function as schools, embassies, workshops, caravans, and oath groups.

## Social Logic

Families earn status by maintaining good remembered relations across many ecological partners. A family that hoards, wounds, or breaks route obligations may still physically travel, but the continent stops opening itself to them.

## Brood Rings

*Ghostlight elaboration for `hearth-brood-ring-v0`.*

A brood ring forms when young Sa'ueia and young allied organisms share a caretaker circuit long enough to learn one another's distress, rest, grooming, and feeding signals. A common ring gathers in a breeding ground's lantern-side warm hollow: glassback calves share stored plate heat and low-grade route confidence through the daily eclipse, while Sa'ueia caretakers protect the calving lane, clean plate seams, prune parasites, and teach children when a readable body has declined inspection. The relationship is neither ownership nor a decorative claim of kinship. It is a period of mutual dependence that leaves obligations after the bodies take different routes.

Handover therefore requires more than transferring an archive. An incoming family member and a returning herd adult must each demonstrate the body-specific care that the young already recognize: how a child's facial fans signal overload, how a calf fogs its plates to request privacy, where either body accepts grooming, and which rest call means that the other should stay close. The young participate. A caretaker who can recite the record but cannot receive a refusal has not received the brood.

The cost follows both parties away. A family unit may owe a detour, parasite work, or defense at the calf's next calving lane; the herd may owe heat shelter, honest-water guidance, or safe passage to the child's later route. Breeding grounds spend food, light, and caretaker sleep holding the overlap long enough for a safe separation. Forced separation can produce calf panic, child distrust, herd route refusal, and a remembered care debt. Refusing eventual separation is also a breach: a brood ring that ignores a maturing calf's herd life or a young Sa'ueia's chosen family route has turned care into custody.
