# Sa'ueia Family Units

Sa'ueia family units are squad-sized mobile groups. They combine kinship, defense, childcare, craft, trade, ritual practice, ecological negotiation, and memory keeping.

## Scale

The unit should be small enough for personal trust and large enough for specialized roles. Wolf-pack comparison is useful for movement, hunting, and kinship pressure, but the units also function as schools, embassies, workshops, caravans, and oath groups.

## Social Logic

Families earn status by maintaining good remembered relations across many ecological partners. A family that hoards, wounds, or breaks route obligations may still physically travel, but the continent stops opening itself to them.

## Future-Kin

A Sa'ueia child does not keep every memory-pattern required for its adult body inside its own tissues. During early care at a [[Ecology/Breeding Grounds|breeding ground]], specialists draw out the most mutation-sensitive unfolding memories: instructions for later organ changes, immune boundaries, sensory calibration, and other developments that would be catastrophic if a single infection rewrote them. Overlapping portions are lodged in three living keepers with unlike immune ecologies. One is usually Sa'ueia; at least one is not.

These keepers are future-kin. A mobile allied organism, a bounded root or fungal body, or another willing [[Species/Ecological Memory Partners|ecological memory partner]] can hold a future without understanding it as a Sa'ueia does. Each carrier feeds, edits, and protects its portion through its own body. None holds enough to dictate maturation alone. When two independently kept portions agree, a growing child's tissues can use their overlap to detect corruption before accepting a returned pattern. Future-keeping is the Sa'ueia answer to a danger created by [[Ecology/Mutable Memory Endosymbiosis]]: the safest place for part of your body may be inside someone too biologically different to forge it cleanly.

This makes kinship physiological without making the child property. Future-kin owe continuing care, truthful disclosure of illness or contamination, and return when a held pattern begins to unfold. The child can refuse a returned pattern, and a keeper can refuse a transfer that its own tissues cannot survive. Ordinary family routes bend around these obligations. A child may depend more directly on a route organism visited at every growth interval than on a genetic contributor they have never met.

A caretaker handover cannot be completed by passing an archive or naming a replacement. For three consecutive eclipse-and-recovery cycles, the departing and incoming keepers both carry the same portion while caring for the child together. The child's tissues learn the incoming body's version and either admit it or reject it. Only after admission may the departing keeper let its copy decay. Skipping the overlap produces a plausible memory with no trusted bodily history, the developmental equivalent of a stranger arriving with an excellent imitation of the family voice.

If too few compatible portions return, the child still grows, but endangered differentiations remain closed. The body assembles an alternate adulthood from what it can verify, often with different sensory emphases, tool needs, symbiont tolerances, and care dependencies. This is not regarded as lesser personhood. It is, however, expensive, intimate, and politically visible. Families can lose a possible future through death, migration, ecological refusal, or a quarrel with someone whose body was part of the nursery.

The central abuse is maturation capture. Any coalition that controls two agreeing keepers can withhold a future, conceal contamination, or return an edited agreement that steers a dependent toward a useful body-role. Sa'ueia law therefore treats collusion between future-kin as an abduction even when nobody has moved. [[Ecology/Breeding Grounds|Breeding grounds]] maintain emergency keepers for children whose kin-web has fractured, but using them makes the commons a bodily relative with claims on later teaching, repair, and return. What looks from outside like caste, adoption, apprenticeship, disability, or citizenship may all be the same unfinished question: who is keeping which part of whom alive?

This arrangement distributes the leverage described in [[Themes/Memory as Hostage]] rather than abolishing it. It also makes the family itself a small [[Ecology/Fractal Eusocial Web|fractal interbeing]]: not a mind that erases its members, but a body whose organs retain refusal.

## Open Questions

- Which non-Sa'ueia species can safely keep which adult developments, and what nourishment, migration, or reproductive access do they demand for doing so?
- Can an adolescent deliberately assemble an adulthood from incompatible held futures, or will their tissues treat the experiment as coercive memory traffic?
- What does the [[Airawa Empire]] do with a people whose bodies remain distributed across citizens and ecologies the empire refuses to recognize as sovereign?
