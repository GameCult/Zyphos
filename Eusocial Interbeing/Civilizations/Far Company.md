# Far Company

*Canon candidate. Association and locality names are provisional translations.*

A threadwing circles back to find its Sa'ueia companions still below the ridge. It has already seen the next valley. They have spent much of the climb arguing about what it will look like. On a Far Company journey, the courier is allowed to keep the surprise.

The Far Company is a small association of [[Sa'ueia Family Units|Sa'ueia travelers]] and [[Threadwing Couriers]] who choose destinations together. Members want to arrive somewhere as guests with interests of their own: to watch an unfamiliar migration, meet a celebrated route reader, or visit the nesting cliffs known to one companion since its first flights. Carrying somebody else's message can take a threadwing far without ever allowing it to choose the visit.

The association gathers along a proposed upland circuit on the [[Sa'ueia Continent]], where several family routes cross a courier corridor between nesting cliffs and lantern groves. It exists during the continent's independent nomadic civilization, before imperial contact. Its first outward ambition is quite specific: a joint visit to the farther cliffs, where these couriers already travel but none of the participating families has been received. They want the threadwing nesting groups there to invite both kinds of traveler as members of one company.

## The Journey They Want

Members enjoy anticipation almost as much as arrival. They make competing predictions about the destination and preserve the confidently wrong ones in retelling. A Sa'ueia route reader is still reminded that the impressive shadow above one ridge turned out to be a nearby vane. The threadwing responsible has repeated the approach several times.

Admired companions know when to hurry and when another body needs them to stop. Threadwings fly and glide on ribbonlike sensory vanes; their partners cannot simply accompany every aerial shortcut. Journeys follow connected ground routes, with agreed places to regroup. Couriers remain responsible for feeding, nests, mates, and living cargo. A recreational excursion cannot make those demands disappear. Some members only join a short stretch. Some enjoy preparing an invitation and hearing what happened more than traveling themselves.

Families continue their ordinary craft, exchange, and ecological work along the way. Couriers continue taking suitable deliveries. These activities sustain the travelers; the association has no separate means of feeding them. A visit is planned where existing work and obligations leave room for it. Young Sa'ueia learn the company's habit of asking what their companion wants to see before announcing where everyone is going.

## Whose Arrival Counts

The company brings together people whose standing usually grows in different circles. A respected family can introduce a courier to partners who have previously treated it only as a useful carrier. A courier can secure a hearing at cliffs where that family's reputation means little. Neither introduction guarantees admission. [[Reciprocal Ecologies|Remembered local relationships]] still determine passage, food, and shelter.

One influential family supports the cliff journey but wants it received as a family embassy, with the couriers thanked as guides. Its members expect to answer for damage caused by their guests and want the standing that comes with that responsibility. Several company couriers refuse this arrangement. They brought the invitation into reach; they intend to be among those welcomed.

Another family, less prominent on the upland circuit, backs the joint arrival. It hopes the company will let it build friendships without always entering behind its powerful neighbor. That support is useful and openly ambitious. The couriers disagree among themselves too: one wants recognition at the cliffs, while its mate values the reliable roosts offered by the embassy's sponsor and sees no attraction in insulting it.

Nearby [[Umbros-Facing Lantern Trees|lantern groves]] can benefit from additional pollinator visits and useful reports. They also control passage and have roots to protect. A grove's willingness to receive the company does not commit the next grove, or turn its resident organisms into association members. The travelers must earn each welcome without claiming to speak for the route beneath them.

## Unresolved Hooks

- The distant hosts invite the couriers by familiar nest affiliations and the Sa'ueia by family. The visit can happen, but the company has not obtained the shared recognition it wanted.
- The sponsoring family offers an excellent alternative destination where everyone will be received together. Accepting would give the company an early success and put its itinerary firmly in that family's hands.
- A courier brings back a request from the cliffs: their hosts want to make a return visit on foot-level routes they have never needed to understand. Someone must now choose what to show them.
