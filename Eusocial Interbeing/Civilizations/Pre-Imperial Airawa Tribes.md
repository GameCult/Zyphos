# Pre-Imperial Airawa Tribes

Before the empire, Airawa tribes formed regional networks around [[Matriarch Trees]], also called mother trees. These trees preserved communal memory, allowing a tribe to retain knowledge beyond the lives of its members. Political life depended on that memory and on relationships with the surrounding ecology. Authority was local, and identities were distributed across ties to particular places and ancestries.

## Living With the Network

These communities were closely integrated with their local ecosystems. They maintained ritualized obligations to non-Airawa partners, making those relationships part of communal life. Craftspeople made the agreements visible: what someone wore or carried could show which ecological contracts protected a person, household, route, or archive duty.

The network gave tribes durable memory and strong ecological connections, but it also gave others ways to control them. Mother trees could withhold memory. Rival tribes could use ecological intermediaries to isolate one another. Fear of breaking continuity with the archives constrained innovation: trying something new could put a community's connection to its remembered past at risk.

Trust held these networks together. Later, that same trust made memetic infection catastrophic.

## Contract Dress

Clothing and equipment helped Airawa communicate their relationships to strangers. Clothing, bindings, ornaments, tool wraps, climbing harnesses, dyes, carved plate inlays, and bioluminescent filigree could identify active agreements with mother trees, pollinator guilds, fungal roads, defensive symbionts, wetland archives, or other local interbeings. Craft served as diplomacy that a person could wear or carry.

A stranger reading these signs could judge which obligations they might disturb through theft, violence, seduction, trade, trespass, or rescue. Even helping someone could involve relationships extending beyond the person in front of them.

Intimidation was one useful purpose of contract dress, but its meanings were broader. A beautiful object could carry a threat, serve as a receipt, preserve a record of mourning, or invite negotiation.

## The Imperial Response

The tribe that founded the empire correctly recognized the coercion in this dependence on the trees and their networks. It then built a worse dependency and called it liberation.
