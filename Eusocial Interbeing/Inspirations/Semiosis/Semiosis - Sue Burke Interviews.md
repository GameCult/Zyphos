# Semiosis - Sue Burke Interviews

## Why These Interviews Matter

Sue Burke’s interviews offer ways to think about plant cognition and colonization: how might people negotiate with nonhuman beings whose lives and decisions unfold over very different timescales?

These are sources of inspiration for this setting, not adopted canon.

## Translation Into This Setting

The [[Matriarch Trees]] should have priorities shaped by their biology. They preserve communal memory, participate directly in Airawa reproduction, and help maintain the ecological networks on which communities depend. Those roles give them ways to compel cooperation: refusing a tree can put access to memory, reproduction, and ecological continuity at risk.

Their own timescales and reproductive strategies should shape those demands. Giving a tree human motives and a wooden body would miss the point.

The [[Sa'ueia]] side should carry this approach beyond plants. Families move among ecological partners that can remember earlier relationships. The continent is not one mind making a single judgment; it holds many remembered relationships.
