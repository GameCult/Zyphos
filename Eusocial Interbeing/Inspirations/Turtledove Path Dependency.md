# Turtledove Path Dependency

## The Reference

Harry Turtledove's "The Road Not Taken" offers a way to think about uneven technological development. A species or civilization may discover a powerful technology early and organize its development around that success. Other kinds of advancement can remain underdeveloped—or never become recognizable possibilities.

This is path dependency: what a civilization has already learned to do shapes what it can imagine doing next.

## Use In This Setting

The [[Airawa Empire]] discovers the [[Biological Memetic Engine]] and develops extraordinary skill in using biology to influence belief, manage heredity, and dominate living networks. Its success narrows how its institutions understand problems. When the engine cannot supply an answer, they misread the problem as contamination, savagery, or impossible nonsense.

The [[Sa'ueia]] provide a comparison through a different civilizational history, rather than through superior technology. Their civilization develops around reciprocal relationships spread across many participants, archives that travel with them, sacred shared reproductive sites, and reputations built through their dealings with other living systems.
