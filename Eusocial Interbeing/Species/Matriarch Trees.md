# Matriarch Trees

Mother Trees are memory-bearing ecological hubs native to the [[Airawa Home Continent]]. They store communal memories for Airawa tribes and coordinate local biological networks through root, fungal, chemical, electrical, and symbiotic channels.

## The Bargain

Pre-imperial Airawa communities depended on mother trees for continuity beyond living memory. In return, the trees demanded nutrients, tending, defense, reproductive assistance, and deference.

This bargain was intimate and coercive at the same time. A community that defied its mother tree risked losing ancestral memory, ecological warning, and legal continuity.

## Authored Elaboration: Recall Audiences

A recall audience is the routine ritual labor by which an Airawa community asks
a matriarch tree to recompose one bounded memory. Petitioners do not address a
passive record. They bring living corroboration: shed skin carrying their own
memory symbionts, a fungal strand that remembers the relevant route or injury,
a threadwing vane fiber or cargo trace from a distant witness, and enough sugar
and minerals to pay for opening the archive tissue. Airawa brace upper claws
and grasping feet into scarred root galleries while keeping their lower hands
free to sort these offerings against contract tokens.

The tree admits the traces through a shallow root chamber. Fungal partners
compare them with stored patterns, courier residues establish where the claim
has traveled, and the tree lets compatible fragments propagate through the
petitioners' memory symbionts. Recall is therefore felt as borrowed reflex,
taste, pain, orientation, and song before it becomes a spoken account. It can
recover a disaster response, route judgment, gestation precedent, or old
betrayal, but it cannot produce an objective view from nowhere. The tree chooses
which pattern to emphasize, and disagreements among witnesses remain palpable
as sensations that will not settle into one body.

Many Umbros-facing groves hold these audiences during eclipse ingress, when
lantern-tree and fungal-road traffic already changes rhythm and the gathering
can be witnessed across species. The timing is customary rather than magical:
other groves use rain pulses, pollinator arrivals, or root-pressure cycles.

Recall consumes stored energy and makes the open archive briefly vulnerable.
Lantern routes may dim, fungal roads may defer other work, and petitioners can
carry an ancestor's fear or motor habit for hours afterward. A missing or bribed
witness can bias the reconstruction; contaminated fungal tissue can smear one
lineage into another; repeated recall can exhaust shallow tissue and force the
tree to seal it. The [[Biological Memetic Engine]] is especially dangerous
during an audience because the same permeability that permits communal memory
also admits authored payloads.

Airawa law, repair work, defense, and reproduction depend on these audiences,
while the tree depends on Airawa maintenance and on partner species willing to
corroborate its archive. A tree may refuse, demand a larger payment, or return a
memory whose emphasis favors its own survival. In many native polities a dead
precedent denied recall has reduced standing. The wonder is real; so is the
leverage.

## Gestation

Matriarch trees are literal Airawa gestators, not only symbolic ancestors or memory
servers. They receive compatible gametes through contracted pollinator species,
manage developmental environments, and bind young Airawa into local symbiont
memory, lineage, and ecological obligation before birth.

This makes reproduction inseparable from politics. An Airawa child is not only
born from parents. They are permitted by a tree, carried through a contract,
delivered through pollinator traffic, and introduced into a network that already
has claims on their future.

## Imperial Rupture

The [[Biological Memetic Engine]] was created to break this dependency. It converted the tree network from negotiated archive into imperial transmission infrastructure.

The engine did not abolish hostage memory. It changed the hostage-taker.
