# Ecological Bargaining

Ecological bargaining means dealing with nonhuman organisms that have memories, reproductive interests, and something others need. Those organisms can negotiate because they have leverage: cooperation depends on what each side gives in return.

For the Airawa, the exchange depended on a rooted archive. They supplied nutrients and obedience in return for continued access to ancestral memory. Preserving that connection to their ancestors meant accepting the demands of the organisms that held it.

The Sa'auei'a bargain works through movement and reputation. Generosity and restraint help secure future access, guidance, and protection. What matters is how past conduct shapes later cooperation, rather than what a community gives to a rooted archive.

Imperial biopolitics tries to replace these exchanges with command, turning ecology into a system the empire can direct. This works until the empire encounters organisms and societies that retain memory without accepting orders.
