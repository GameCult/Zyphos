# Technological Path Dependency

Technological path dependency is what happens when a civilization's early successes shape how it tackles later problems. Institutions organize around what works, and people become skilled at using it. Over time, they can struggle to imagine solutions that require a different approach.

The [[Airawa Empire]] is the main example. Its [[Biological Memetic Engine]] can change beliefs and behavior through living networks. It proved so effective that every imperial institution came to depend on it. The empire became extraordinarily capable at controlling populations through biology and belief, but poor at cooperation with partners whose wishes it cannot overwrite.

The [[Sa'ueia]] show a different set of strengths and limits. Their way of life favors movement, reciprocal relationships, and shared breeding grounds. These habits make them resilient against imperial capture, but leave them less able to mobilize quickly under central command.

As a worldbuilding theme, path dependency asks how a successful tool changes a society's judgment. The point is not that technology is bad: a tool can deliver real benefits while teaching its users to recognize only certain kinds of answer as a solution.
