# Airawa Home Continent

Across the Airawa home continent, trees connect dense networks that hold and exchange memory. Before imperial consolidation, each major region had its own [[Matriarch Trees]], which stored communal memory and coordinated local ecology. Airawa tribes shared these networks with pollinator species, mycorrhizal archives, scavenger guilds, and defensive symbionts. Local agreements governed their relationships.

One tribe's invention of the [[Biological Memetic Engine]] broke that political order. The engine could spread ideas and alter memory and behavior through living networks. Its inventors discovered that any problem the tree network could reach could be addressed by dominating those involved, editing them, or infecting them with doctrine. The network that supported local agreements became a means of imposing imperial control.

## Present Condition

The [[Airawa Empire]] controls roughly one quarter of the continent. Its official maps depict a unified, civilized ecology expanding into uncivilized remnants. This is the empire's account of the continent, not an accurate description of who lives beyond its borders.

The remaining three quarters contain native [[Nation Scale Interbeings]]: territorial, multispecies systems that carry memory and act as political bodies. They take many forms:

- mother-tree archival polities;
- disconnected resistance networks;
- migratory route bodies;
- fungal-road systems;
- wetland confederacies;
- reef-linked coasts;
- local Airawa societies that never accepted the empire's organization of living networks as liberation.

Several [[Disconnected Tree Networks]] survived by cutting their connections before the engine could spread fully through their living systems. Other native nations were never organized as branches of the network the empire took over. They are harder for the empire to fit into its categories, and harder to conquer by gaining control of a single root.

The empire denies that these independent networks exist. Acknowledging them would mean admitting that the engine can be resisted, that the continent was not willingly unified, and that imperial mind control, however powerful, is not total.
