# The Returning Jaws

*Canon candidate. The locality and its translated names are provisional.*

At Splitwater, an inlet on the [[World/Sa'ueia Continent|Sa'ueia continent]] before imperial contact, a wounded hunter can rest inside the same reef that sheltered an animal it recently pursued. The chambers are separate. The water passing through them belongs to one colony, and the colony has chosen to keep both animals alive.

Several [[Biosphere/Aquatic Colony Feeders/Choir Reefs|choir reefs]] here cultivate this practice. Their anchored bodies consist of small filtering organisms sharing mineral skeletons; they can open shelters, change currents, and answer visitors through pressure and mineral taste. Among these neighbours, a particularly admired shelter is one from which a badly injured swimmer eventually departs under its own power. Visiting [[Sa'ueia]] families call the practice “returning the jaws” when the survivor happens to have teeth.

The local hunters, provisionally called cleft hunters, have flattened swimming bodies, paired gripping jaws, and pressure-sensitive strips along their flanks. They find small swimmers through water disturbance and traces left by wounded tissue. Repeated feeding teaches them which approaches yield prey. Nothing in their observed behaviour requires understanding a sanctuary, gratitude, or a promise. A hunter released from shelter may immediately pursue another departing patient.

The reefs know this. Ordinarily they release vulnerable swimmers through narrow inner passages and hunters through an outer cleft. They can make pursuit difficult within their own channels; beyond them, the water offers no continuing guarantee. The arrangement persists because injury need not be fatal, because returning visitors bring mineral gifts and corpses, and because their passing bodies carry impressions of places an anchored reef cannot visit. A hunter's successful recovery also restores a predator to the inlet. Reefs differ over how much weight that consequence deserves.

For the colony called Long Opening, sheltering a wide variety of bodies is a continuing ambition. It varies the flow around resting swimmers, attending to the difference between a body that can hold its position and one being carried helplessly against the skeleton. Coastal families recognize its satisfaction in a familiar low pressure sequence after a difficult recovery. That interpretation has grown through repeated visits; the reef supplies no spoken account of its feelings. Families bring accounts of survivors encountered farther along the coast. A dull journey can become worth reporting because a particular torn flank has healed.

Its neighbour, Close Teeth, keeps excellent shelters for smaller swimmers and turns large hunters away. It has fewer dramatic recoveries to report and more small bodies passing safely through. Their disagreement is visible in which openings admit whom. Neither colony needs a council chamber to make its preference inconvenient to the other.

A storm has now packed Long Opening's inner passages with rubble. Small patients must leave through the outer cleft. One recovered hunter has begun circling there, returning after the reef drives it away with disorienting pressure. The hunter remembers successful feeding; the reef remembers saving it. Those memories do not constitute an agreement.

Long Opening has stopped releasing its smallest patients. Holding them consumes shelter space and keeps hungry bodies in a place intended for recovery. Reopening the inner passages would require coastal helpers to move rubble near living skeleton and occupied chambers. Close Teeth is opening space of its own, but transferring swimmers across the exposed water gives the hunter another opportunity.

Some returning families want Long Opening to lure the hunter away toward a rocky reach without a resident reef. Others point out that they have not established what feeds there. Moving a hunt can save familiar bodies while making unfamiliar bodies easier to catch. Long Opening continues to shelter a second injured hunter in an outer chamber. Driving this patient out now would be a choice about this animal, before anybody has settled the larger argument.

- The second hunter begins feeding normally and pressing toward its chamber opening while the first still circles outside.
- A family offers to clear the inner passage, but only after moving the patients whose bodies would be closest to the work.
- Close Teeth receives returning swimmers that once went to Long Opening; their gifts and route impressions begin following them.
